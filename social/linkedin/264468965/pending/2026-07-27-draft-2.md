If you're still thinking about AI as "one model, one task" — you're already behind.

The real shift? Agents that talk to each other.

One agent researches. Another analyzes. A third drafts recommendations. A fourth validates against compliance rules.

All running in parallel. All coordinated. All shipping output that used to take a team of analysts.

This isn't sci-fi. This is what winning teams are deploying TODAY.

The question isn't whether multi-agent architectures work. It's whether your infrastructure can handle them.

Orchestration. Observability. The ability to debug a chain of 12 agents when something goes sideways.

Single-agent thinking got us here. Multi-agent thinking gets us to the next level.

What would your workflow look like with 10 agents instead of 1?
