They settled on $5B at a $190B valuation.

Think about that. Databricks came to the table asking for $1B. Investors pushed $15B across the table. The gap between what founders think they need and what the market sees? Massive.

Here's what this tells you about fundraising:

The best companies don't chase valuations. They build traction so undeniable that investors chase them.

Databricks didn't negotiate up from $1B to $5B because they were greedy. They got there because the numbers backed it. Revenue, growth, market position — all pointing in one direction.

The lesson: when you're fundraising, you're not convincing investors your company is worth X. You're showing them the math that proves it's worth at least X. Maybe more.

Are you asking for what you need, or what the evidence supports?
