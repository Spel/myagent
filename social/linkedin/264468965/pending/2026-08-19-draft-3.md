The question: are VCs holding board seats at multiple competing startups? And is that anticompetitive?

The VCs are baffled. They say board seats are how they help founders. Taking them away hurts the companies.

But here's the tension: when one firm sits on the boards of five AI companies doing similar things, who benefits? The startups? Or the firm that gets to see everyone's cards?

The lesson: power concentrates until someone pushes back. The VC model assumes alignment between firm and founder. But the more positions you hold, the harder alignment becomes.

This isn't just regulatory noise. It's a question about whose interests board seats actually serve.

What conflicts of interest are hiding in plain sight in your industry?
