The former Uber CEO just kicked off another round of VC bashing. His point: most VCs add noise, not value. Only 1% actually help founders build better companies.

Strong take. But here's the nuance: helpful depends on what you need.

Some founders need capital and nothing else. They want to be left alone. For them, a hands-off VC is ideal.

Other founders need introductions, hiring help, strategic guidance. They want a partner. For them, the "helpful" VC is worth the premium.

The problem isn't VCs being helpful or not. The problem is the mismatch. Founders who want help getting passive capital. Founders who want autonomy getting meddlesome boards.

The lesson: before you take money, get clarity. What do you actually need from your investors? And can they deliver it?

VCs aren't all the same. Neither are founders. Match deliberately.
