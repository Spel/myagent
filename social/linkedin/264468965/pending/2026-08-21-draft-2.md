Runlayer and Rippling were locked in a legal battle. Now they've both walked away.

The details are messy — allegations of stolen data, poached employees, competitive sabotage. The kind of drama that makes headlines and burns cash.

But here's what matters for founders: lawsuits are a tax on momentum.

Every hour your leadership spends in depositions is an hour not spent on product. Every dollar going to lawyers is a dollar not going to growth. Win or lose, litigation is a distraction tax.

Sometimes you have to fight. But most of the time, the best move is to build something so good that the competition becomes irrelevant.

The lesson: protect what matters. But don't let protection become your primary activity. The best defense in startups is offense.

Are you spending more time defending or attacking?
