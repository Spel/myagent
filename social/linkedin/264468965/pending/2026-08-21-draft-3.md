𝗧𝗵𝗲 "𝗘𝗻𝗵𝗮𝗻𝗰𝗲𝗱 𝗚𝗮𝗺𝗲𝘀" — 𝘁𝗲𝗰𝗵'𝘀 𝘀𝘁𝗲𝗿𝗼𝗶𝗱 𝗲𝘅𝘁𝗿𝗮𝘃𝗮𝗴𝗮𝗻𝘇𝗮 — 𝗽𝗼𝘀𝘁𝗲𝗱 𝗮 $𝟲𝟬𝗠 𝗹𝗼𝘀𝘀.

The pitch was bold: an Olympics-style event where athletes could use performance enhancements. Tech meets sports. Innovation meets competition.

The reality? A $60 million loss. The experiment didn't pay off.

Here's the uncomfortable truth about bold experiments: most of them fail. Not because the idea was wrong. But because the market wasn't ready, the timing wasn't right, or the execution wasn't there.

The Enhanced Games tested a hypothesis: what if we let athletes push the limits? The market answered: we're not interested.

The lesson: disruption requires more than a provocative idea. It requires timing, narrative, and a audience ready to be disrupted.

Bold ideas are easy. Bold ideas that land are rare. The difference is usually in the details, not the thesis.

What hypothesis are you testing — and how will you know if it's working?
