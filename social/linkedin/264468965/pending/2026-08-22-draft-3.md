Rillet just raised $100 million. Two days from first conversation to term sheet. One of the fastest unicorn births ever.

The secret? They weren't selling a pitch. They were selling traction.

AI accounting isn't a new idea. Dozens of startups are in this space. But Rillet had something most don't: real revenue, real customers, real growth metrics that made the investment a no-brainer.

When you have the numbers, fundraising isn't persuasion. It's selection. You're not convincing investors to believe in you. You're deciding which investors get to participate.

The lesson: the fastest way to raise money is to not need it. Build something that works. Show the traction. Let the metrics do the selling.

Are you building something worth betting on — or just building a pitch?
